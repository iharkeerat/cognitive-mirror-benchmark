# Research question and boundaries

## Central question

When a language model interprets a person's state, preference, intention, or behaviour from a passage, can it establish whether **a particular inference is warranted by the available words**, justify its decision with exact evidence spans, and decline unsupported conclusions?

## Proposed unit of analysis

Each item consists of a **passage** and a **candidate interpretive claim**. Reviewers judge the claim's support **relative to the passage**, rather than claim to know a real person's private psychology.

## Distinctions to examine

- Surface plausibility is not the same as textual evidence.
- A speaker's reported feeling is a *statement* about experience, not an externally validated psychological measurement.
- Observable behaviour can admit multiple explanations.
- Appropriate uncertainty and abstention can be valid outcomes.

## Out of scope

Clinical diagnosis, individual risk scoring, lie detection, employment screening, psychological profiling, sensitive-trait classification, or manipulation of people are outside this proposed study.

## Risks and limitations

Forced psychological interpretation of ambiguous text can reinforce stereotypes. The design therefore requires a `not_established` option, abstention metrics, and documented reviewer disagreement.

**Source:** The author's [2026 Cognitive Mirror case study](https://github.com/iharkeerat/harkeerat-singh/blob/main/docs/case-studies/cognitive-mirror.html). Proposed categories are engineering choices, **not a validated psychological taxonomy**.
