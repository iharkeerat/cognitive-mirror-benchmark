# Contributing and reviewing

The most useful contributions are **constructive methodological critiques**: clearer operational definitions, ethical safeguards, better uncertainty scoring, annotation disagreements, leakage controls and ways to falsify unsupported conclusions.

Do not submit: real identifiable personal passages, private conversations, mental-health records, student files, employee assessments or clinical labels. Do not claim an institutional/ethical approval that is not documented.

For proposed edits:

1. Identify the exact section and a specific issue.
2. State whether this is a factual correction, normative proposal or testable hypothesis.
3. Explain what evidence or review would settle it.
4. Preserve current unresolved status and uncertainty rather than filling in speculative results.

No example is eligible for real-study annotation without source rights and ethics/privacy clearance.
